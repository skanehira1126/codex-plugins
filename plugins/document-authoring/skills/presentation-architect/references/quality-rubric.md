# Presentation quality rubric

Score each dimension 0–2 after the storyboard and again after rendered production.

| Dimension | 0 — failure | 1 — partial | 2 — strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Audience task and intended outcome | Audience or purpose is unknown | Purpose exists but the intended outcome is vague | Audience A → B, meeting use, and any applicable ask or next use are explicit |
| 2. Governing message and progression | Only a topic or disconnected content | A message or objective exists but progression is weak | One governing message or objective controls a coherent argument, learning progression, or update path |
| 3. Core-deck selection | Core includes everything or omits essentials | Some triage, but several slides do not earn time | Core contains only what everyone must experience to reach the intended outcome |
| 4. Storyline and sequence | Slides are source-order topics or contain jumps | Most transitions are inferable | Titles alone tell a coherent argument, learning progression, or update path, and every slide follows for a reason |
| 5. Cognitive and visual rhythm | Long runs of identical explanatory slides or layouts | Some variation, partly decorative | Audience operations and visual forms vary with semantic purpose |
| 6. Slide message and support | Multiple messages, generic titles, or unsupported content | Primary message exists but evidence, example, or explanation is weak or distant | One message per slide; the body supports the title with the profile-appropriate evidence, example, explanation, or action |
| 7. Visual-semantic hierarchy | Layout is decorative, flat, or misleading | Legible but generic | Spatial hierarchy, chart, or diagram directly explains the relationship |
| 8. Appendix and retrieval readiness | Material questions or verification needs are unsupported, or appendix hides critical content | Some support exists but retrieval, coverage, or scope is weak | Material needs are covered by core or a question-driven appendix; no appendix is created when none is useful |
| 9. Evidence and trust | Claims are unsupported, invented, or overconfident | Evidence exists but citations or caveats are weak | Sources are traceable and certainty matches support |
| 10. Artifact and rehearsal quality | File is broken, unreadable, or untested | Usable with minor defects or timing risk | Deck is rendered, filmstrip-reviewed, retains required editability, is legible, and fits delivery |

## Passing rule

- Use the hard gates below, not the total score, to decide whether a deck passes.
- Dimensions 1–9 are mandatory; none may score 0.
- Treat the total as a directional editorial aid, not a calibrated or objective quality measure.
- A rough storyboard may score lower only when explicitly requested and labeled.

## Hard gates

Fail the deck until repaired when any applies:

- the intended outcome or governing message/objective is not clear from the core sequence, or an applicable exact ask is missing;
- core titles alone do not tell the story;
- a slide contains multiple competing primary messages;
- outcome-changing evidence, risk, limitation, or assumption appears only in notes or appendix;
- the core sequence cannot fit the time budget without rushing the intended audience work or using tiny text;
- several consecutive slides require only absorbing dense abstract explanation;
- material questions or verification needs, when present, cannot be answered quickly from the core deck or appendix;
- the rendered deck contains clipping, unreadable text, broken charts, inconsistent notes, or navigation defects.

## Review severity

- **Critical**: likely to cause a wrong decision, factual misunderstanding, or failed presentation.
- **Major**: materially weakens attention, progression, evidence, learning, or retrieval readiness.
- **Minor**: local clarity, wording, consistency, or polish issue.
