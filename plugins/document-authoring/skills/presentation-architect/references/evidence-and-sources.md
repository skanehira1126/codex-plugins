# Evidence, sources, and trust calibration for slides

## Classify claims

- **Fact**: directly supported by reliable evidence.
- **Interpretation**: a reasoned reading of facts.
- **Hypothesis**: a testable explanation with incomplete support.
- **Proposal**: a recommended choice or action.

Do not let a confident visual treatment turn a hypothesis into an apparent fact.

## Slide-level sourcing

- Put source notes close enough to trace the claim.
- Preserve date, population, denominator, definition, and comparison for numbers.
- Label provisional or illustrative figures.
- Distinguish event date from publication date.
- Verify time-sensitive facts.
- Do not cite a topically related source unless it supports the exact proposition.
- Never invent URLs, quotes, footnotes, page numbers, or data.

## Chart integrity

- Use the correct denominator and baseline.
- Avoid truncated axes or visual encodings that exaggerate differences.
- Label the decisive value or comparison directly.
- Keep caveats on the core slide when they change interpretation; use appendix for method detail.
- Match chart title to the claim the data actually supports.

## Evidence gaps

Use explicit language:

- `Evidence gap: baseline is not yet measured.`
- `Hypothesis: approval, not analysis, is the dominant delay.`
- `Decision under uncertainty: run a reversible pilot.`

A compelling storyline does not justify filling a gap with invented evidence.
